# Making water safe to drink in an emergency

Summary of the public guidance of the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), "Making Water Safe in an Emergency". Check the current version of official guidance where you are.

## Boiling

Boiling is the surest method to kill disease-causing germs. If the water is cloudy, let it settle and filter it through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter first.

Bring the water to a rolling boil for at least one minute. At elevations above 2,000 metres (6,500 feet), boil for three minutes. Let the water cool naturally and store it in clean, covered containers.

## Disinfecting with household bleach

If boiling is not possible, use unscented liquid household chlorine bleach. Check the label: it must be intended for disinfection or sanitisation, and you need its sodium hypochlorite concentration.

For bleach with 6 % sodium hypochlorite, add 8 drops (about half a millilitre) per 4 litres (one gallon) of water. Double the amount if the water is cloudy, coloured or very cold. Stir and let it stand for at least 30 minutes before use.

## Storing water

Store at least 4 litres (one gallon) of water per person per day, for several days. Use food-grade containers, keep them in a cool, dark place, and replace stored tap water every six months.
